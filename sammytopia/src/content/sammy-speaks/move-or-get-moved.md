# SAMMY SPEAKS
## MOVE OR GET MOVED

There are two kinds of people standing at a blocked road. One waits for the road to open. The other starts looking for a way through.

I have learned that sometimes, if you don't make a move, something else will make it for you.

Move—or get moved.

I did not invent that idea from a comfortable place. I learned it during a period when my own plans stopped moving.

After secondary school, I expected the next step to be university. That was the direction I had been looking toward. But life does not always respect the timetable we create for it.

Financial circumstances interrupted my plans.

For a moment, it felt as though I had reached a roadblock. And roadblocks have a strange way of making time feel heavier. A month becomes another month. A plan becomes a question. A question becomes uncertainty.

But I did not want my gap year to become a waiting room.

So I moved.

Not necessarily in the direction I had originally planned, but in a direction that kept me growing.

I returned to the school environment as a teaching assistant. Instead of sitting outside education waiting for my own opportunity to continue, I stepped back into education from another side. I became someone helping students learn while I was still learning myself.

There was something beautiful about that contradiction. I was waiting for my next chapter, but I was also writing it.

Teaching forced me to look at knowledge differently. Knowing something is one thing. Explaining it to another person is another. A concept that seems obvious inside your own head can become surprisingly complicated when someone looks at you and asks, “Why?”

That question made me think. It made me research. It made me return to the foundations of English and examine things I had previously taken for granted.

And while I was teaching, I continued creating.

Writing. Researching. Developing ideas. Thinking about the things I wanted to build in the future.

The year I could have described as “the year I didn't go to university” gradually became something else: the year I refused to stop moving.

That doesn't mean every day was easy. It wasn't. There were moments when I wondered how long I would have to wait.

But I began to understand that movement does not always mean physically changing your location. Sometimes movement means learning something. Sometimes it means creating something. Sometimes it means helping someone else. Sometimes it means taking an idea that has been sitting inside your head for months and finally giving it a shape.

And sometimes movement means confronting whatever is standing in your way.

That is another meaning hidden inside my phrase.

Move or get moved.

If something is blocking my path, I cannot spend forever asking it politely to disappear. I have to find another route. I have to climb over it. I have to go around it. And sometimes, I have to move it.

Not out of anger. Out of necessity.

Because the road ahead belongs to no one but those willing to walk it.

My gap year taught me that a delayed beginning is still a beginning. It taught me that being temporarily stopped does not mean being defeated.

Most importantly, it taught me that I have a choice whenever circumstances interrupt my plans: I can allow the interruption to define me. Or I can use the space it created.

So I keep moving.

Not because I know exactly where every road leads. But because I have learned something about standing still.

If you don't make your move, life eventually makes one for you.

And I would rather choose my direction.

Move—or get moved.

— Samuel Joshua Jason Pratt

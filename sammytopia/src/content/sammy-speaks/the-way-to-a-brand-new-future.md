# SAMMY SPEAKS
## THE WAY TO A BRAND NEW FUTURE

I used to think the future was a place you arrived at. A distant country on the other side of today. A place where everything finally made sense, where the road stopped bending, and where the things I imagined as a child would somehow be waiting for me.

But growing up taught me something different.

The future is not a place. It is a road we build while walking.

For a long time, I wondered what that road would look like for me. I had dreams that seemed too large for the space around me. I wanted to write. I wanted to create. I wanted to perform. I wanted to build things that could outlive the moment in which I imagined them.

Sometimes, however, imagination is easier than action.

It is easy to sit beneath a tree and imagine a magnificent library. It is harder to look at a locked library and ask why it has remained locked. It is easy to complain about what is missing. It is harder to decide that perhaps you can do something about it.

That difference between imagining and doing became important to me.

I began to understand that opportunities do not always arrive wearing the clothes we expect. Sometimes an opportunity looks like a problem. Sometimes it looks like an empty classroom, a locked door, a forgotten idea, or a question nobody seems interested in answering. And sometimes, it looks like having to begin with almost nothing.

My own journey has been filled with such moments.

I learned to make use of what was available to me. When resources were limited, I searched. When I did not know something, I researched it. When I had an idea, I tried to turn it into something tangible.

That mindset followed me into my writing. I began developing books and articles. I experimented with stories. I wrote songs. I organized events. I taught other students. I created projects that existed first in my imagination and then, slowly, began to exist outside it.

Not everything worked. Some ideas remained ideas. Some projects were unfinished. Some doors remained closed.

But I learned that a closed door does not necessarily mean the end of a journey. Sometimes it is simply an invitation to look for another entrance.

My academic journey also taught me that the road to the future is rarely straight. There are moments when you move forward quickly and moments when you seem to be standing still. There are moments when you have to wait, rethink your plans, and begin again.

I have experienced that kind of pause myself.

But I have come to see a pause differently.

A pause is not the same thing as an ending. Sometimes the road needs time to reveal its next turn.

The future I once imagined as a distant destination has therefore become something much more personal. It is in the books I write, the students I teach, the songs I compose, the stories I create, and the ideas I continue to carry around with me.

Perhaps that is what growing up really means: realizing that you may not be able to see the entire road, but you can still take the next step.

And perhaps the brand-new future I have been looking for was never waiting somewhere ahead of me.

Perhaps I have been building it all along.

— Samuel Joshua Jason Pratt
